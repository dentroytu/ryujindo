店A card shop in a Tokyo neighbourhood

A first-person shop sim where the stock is trading cards, and where the money isn't in restocking shelves: it's in knowing what the thing in front of you is worth.

You start in the six-tatami room your grandmother lends you, boxes still taped shut and rent due on Friday. One of those boxes holds a card worth a great deal more than anyone is about to pay you for it. That's the prologue, and everything else comes out of it.

From there the loop is any shop's — order stock, restock, open, take payment, close, pay the rent — with one difference: every card that passes through your hands is worth a different number depending on who buys it. The wholesaler on the corner pays 55 % and pays today. The app pays more and takes its cut, plus postage and a sleeve. A collector in the street overpays for his own set, and the request board pays a premium for the exact card somebody has spent weeks hunting. Choosing the channel is the game.

The goal is in plain sight from day one: the 貸店舗 around the corner — an empty unit, shutter down, "to let" sign in the window. Four acts later, that shutter is yours.

品What's in it

札540 cards, and all of them mean something

Six sets of ninety, each card with its element, its health, its attack and the weakness that takes it down. They come from a deterministic catalogue: 090/090 of the 龍神 set is the same card in your game as in anyone else's.

相場Appraise before you sell

The price isn't a fixed number: condition, foil, set index and Sunday's tournament all move it — the winning element gains 35 %. An appraisal costs money, and getting it wrong costs more.

交換Trading, face to face

At Saturday's market you trade with people who value by rarity and ignore the meta. That's where the margin is — and the person across the table has an eye too.

勝負An actual card game

一本勝負: twenty-card decks, three copies maximum, and never more copies than you really own. Played on the shop tables and at the Sunday tournament.

街A neighbourhood you walk

Nakano, Ameyoko and Akihabara, the covered arcade, the crossing, the station and its platform, a park with cherry trees, and a residential street where nothing is for sale. You walk from one end to the other.

四幕A campaign in four acts

From the room full of moving boxes to signing the lease, with a neighbour after the same unit who is saving up at his own pace. There is an ending, and you can reach it.
